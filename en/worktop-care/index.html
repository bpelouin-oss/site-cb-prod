The right care routine to hand over to your client

Good worktop care comes down to a few simple habits, as long as you know the material. Here is our general advice for each main family, to pass on to your client when you hand over the project. It complements, without replacing, the manufacturer's recommendations for the range, which we provide on request.

Natural stone: granite, marble and quartzite

Granite, marble and quartzite are cleaned day to day with lukewarm water, a mild pH-neutral soap and a soft cloth or non-abrasive sponge. Drying afterwards prevents limescale marks.

Marble needs the most attention. It is sensitive to acids: lemon juice, vinegar, wine, fizzy drinks and some household products can dull its surface. Your client should wipe up these splashes quickly, before they have time to act.

A water-repellent sealer is recommended on marble and many granites to limit stain penetration. How often it should be renewed depends on the stone and its use: point your client to the manufacturer's guidance or ask us. Sensa treated stones, whether granite or quartzite, are protected at the factory and do not need regular sealing.

Quartz: easy, but mind the heat

Quartz is one of the easiest surfaces to live with. Non-porous, it needs no sealing and resists stains very well. A damp cloth with a little washing-up liquid or a mild cleaner is enough in the vast majority of cases. For a dried-on stain, leave soapy water to work for a few minutes before wiping.

Its weak point is heat. The resins that bind quartz do not cope well with sudden thermal shock: explain to your client that they should always use a trivet under a pan or a dish straight from the oven, and never leave a heat-producing appliance directly on the worktop.

Very harsh products, such as strippers, neat bleach or oven cleaners, should be avoided, as should abrasive sponges that could dull the polish. The recommendations of Silestone®, Compac, Stone Italiana or MStone apply depending on the range.

Ceramic: the most tolerant surface

Ceramics such as Dekton®, The Size, Materia, Infinity or MStone are non-porous and need no treatment at all. They resist heat, stains, UV and most household products, which makes them the easiest to maintain in our comparison.

Day to day, hot water and a mild cleaner are enough. On a matt or textured finish, a slightly damp microfibre cloth removes fingerprints and water rings more effectively. For stubborn residue, a mildly abrasive cream is generally tolerated, but the manufacturer's recommendations for the range should be checked first.

Its great hardness has a downside your client should know about: cutting directly on ceramic blunts knives, hence the value of a chopping board. Heavy knocks to the edges with a weighty object, such as a cast-iron pan, should also be avoided, as an edge is more vulnerable than the surface.

Corian® and Minéralis: easy surfaces to live with

Corian® is solid throughout and non-porous. Soapy water or a mild cleaner, applied in circular movements with a sponge, is all it takes to keep it clean. It is more sensitive to scratches and heat than stone: a chopping board and a trivet are essential. Its great advantage is that it can be repaired, as a surface scratch can be removed by the user with the right products, or by a professional depending on its depth.

Minéralis, distributed by MStone Surfaces, is also very easy to clean with water and mild soap. It withstands up to 180 °C, but a trivet is still recommended for dishes straight from the oven or hob.

For both materials, strong solvents and strippers must be avoided. Give your client the manufacturer's care sheet, which we send you with the range documentation.

Habits to avoid on every worktop

Whatever the material, a few habits noticeably extend a worktop's good looks. Here is the list to hand to your client:

  • do not put a hot pan or dish down without a trivet;
  • do not cut directly on the surface, use a chopping board;
  • do not use steel wool, abrasive powder or strippers;
  • avoid neat bleach, acidic descalers and oven cleaners, especially on natural stone;
  • do not let coloured or acidic liquids dry, such as coffee, wine, oil or lemon;
  • do not climb or sit on an overhanging top, or drop heavy objects on the edges;
  • do not mix different household products.

If in doubt about a product, it is best to test it on a hidden area first. The manufacturer's recommendations for the range remain the reference, and we are always available for any technical question.

Your questions

What should your client use to clean a quartz worktop?

A little washing-up liquid diluted in lukewarm water and a soft cloth are enough day to day. For grease marks, a mild non-abrasive household cleaner does the job. Very alkaline or acidic products, neat bleach and abrasive sponges should be avoided, then the surface rinsed and dried. The quartz manufacturer's advice completes these rules.

What should I tell a client who has stained a marble worktop?

That they need to act fast: blot without rubbing, then clean with water and a pH-neutral soap, never using vinegar or an acidic product. If the mark is a dull ring left by an acid, it is surface etching: it will then need restoring by a stone professional, either you or your partner stonemason.

Should I recommend sealing a granite worktop?

It depends on the granite. Some are more porous than others and gain from a water-repellent sealer, renewed according to use and the manufacturer's guidance. A simple test to pass on: a drop of water that darkens the stone within a few minutes shows that sealing is worthwhile. Sensa granites are protected at the factory and need no regular sealing.
