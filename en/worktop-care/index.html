Looking after your worktop, material by material

Good worktop care comes down to a few simple habits, as long as you know your material. Here is our general advice for each main family. It complements, but does not replace, the manufacturer's recommendations for your range, which we pass on to you at fitting.

Caring for a natural stone worktop

Granite, marble and quartzite can be cleaned every day with warm water, a mild pH-neutral soap and a soft cloth or non-abrasive sponge. Dry afterwards to avoid limescale marks.

Marble needs the most attention. It is sensitive to acids: lemon juice, vinegar, wine, fizzy drinks and some household products can dull its surface. Wipe up these splashes quickly, before they have time to react.

A sealing treatment is recommended for marble and many granites to limit how far stains can penetrate. How often to renew it depends on the stone and how it is used: follow the manufacturer's guidance or ask us. Sensa-treated stones, granite or quartzite, are protected at the factory and do not need regular sealing.

Quartz worktops: easy care, but mind the heat

Quartz is one of the easiest surfaces to live with. Being non-porous, it needs no sealing and resists stains very well. A damp cloth with a little washing-up liquid or a mild cleaner is enough in the vast majority of cases. For a dried-on mark, let soapy water sit for a few minutes before wiping.

Its weak point is heat. The resins that bind quartz do not cope well with sudden thermal shock: always put a trivet under a pan or a dish straight from the oven, and avoid leaving a hot appliance directly on the worktop.

Steer clear of very harsh products such as strippers, neat bleach or oven cleaners, as well as abrasive pads that could dull the polish. Refer to the recommendations from Silestone®, Compac, Stone Italiana or MStone, depending on your range.

Ceramic: the most forgiving surface

Ceramics such as Dekton®, The Size, Materia, Infinity and MStone are non-porous and need no treatment at all. They resist heat, stains, UV light and most household products, which makes them the easiest to care for in our comparison.

Day to day, hot water and a mild cleaner are enough. On a matt or textured finish, a slightly damp microfibre cloth is better at lifting fingerprints and water marks. For stubborn residue, a mildly abrasive cream is generally tolerated, but check your range manufacturer's recommendations first.

Its great hardness has a downside: cutting directly on ceramic will blunt your knives. Use a chopping board. Also avoid hard knocks to the edges with heavy objects such as cast iron pans, as an edge is more exposed than the surface.

Corian® and Minéralis: easy-living composite surfaces

Corian® is solid all the way through and non-porous. Soapy water or a mild cleaner, applied with a sponge in circular movements, is all it needs. It is more sensitive to scratches and heat than stone, so always use a chopping board and a trivet. Its great advantage is that it can be repaired: a surface scratch can be smoothed out, either by you with suitable products or by a professional, depending on its depth.

Minéralis, distributed by MStone Surfaces, is also very simple to clean with water and mild soap. It withstands up to 180 °C, but a trivet is still recommended for dishes straight from the oven or hob.

For both materials, avoid strong solvents and strippers, and follow the manufacturer's care instructions.

What to avoid on every worktop

Whatever the material, a few habits go a long way towards keeping your worktop beautiful:

  • do not put a hot pan or dish down without a trivet;
  • do not cut directly on the surface, use a board;
  • do not use wire wool, scouring powder or strippers;
  • avoid neat bleach, acidic limescale removers and oven cleaners, especially on natural stone;
  • do not let coloured or acidic liquids dry, such as coffee, wine, oil or lemon;
  • do not stand or sit on an overhanging section, or drop heavy objects on the edges;
  • do not mix different household products.

If in doubt about a product, test it on a hidden area or ask us. The manufacturer's recommendations for your range remain the reference.

Your questions

What should I use to clean a quartz worktop?

A little washing-up liquid diluted in warm water and a soft cloth are enough for everyday cleaning. For greasy marks, a mild non-abrasive household cleaner will do. Avoid strongly alkaline or acidic products, neat bleach and abrasive pads. Rinse and dry for a streak-free finish, and check the care advice from your quartz manufacturer.

How do I remove a stain from a marble worktop?

Act fast: blot without rubbing, then clean with water and pH-neutral soap. Never use vinegar or any acidic product, which would attack the stone. If the mark is a dull ring left by an acid, the surface itself has been etched: contact us or a stone professional for suitable restoration.

Does a granite worktop need sealing?

It depends on the granite. Some are more porous than others and benefit from a sealing treatment, renewed according to use and the manufacturer's guidance. A simple test: if a drop of water darkens the stone within a few minutes, sealing will help. Sensa granites are protected at the factory and need no regular sealing.
