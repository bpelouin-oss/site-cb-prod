How to choose your worktop material

Start with how you will use the space rather than the colour. A kitchen where you cook every day, with pans put down in a hurry and children around, calls for a different material from a guest bathroom or a dining table.

Four criteria make the difference:

  • scratch resistance, for chopping and for objects slid across the surface;
  • heat resistance, next to the hob;
  • sensitivity to stains: wine, coffee, oil, lemon;
  • day-to-day care, with or without sealing.

Our comparison scores each family on these points. It is only a guide: the finish and thickness also change how a surface behaves. A high-gloss polish, for example, shows fingerprints and water marks more than a honed or leathered finish. The surest way is still to see and touch the slabs.

Natural stone or engineered surfaces?

Natural stone (granite, marble, quartzite) comes straight from the quarry. Every slab is unique, with veining that is never repeated. Granite and quartzite, with a Mohs hardness of 6 to 7, stand up well to heat and scratches. Marble, softer and sensitive to acids, needs sealing and is at its best in bathrooms and statement pieces.

Engineered surfaces work differently. Quartz (Silestone®, Compac, Stone Italiana, MStone) is non-porous and very easy to look after. Ceramic (Dekton®, The Size, Infinity, MStone) scores highest for resistance to scratches, heat and stains. Corian® stands out for its integrated basins and repairable surface. Between the two worlds, Sensa and Texta treated granites and quartzites keep the look of natural stone with fewer constraints.

More responsible materials

Several ranges in our catalogue reduce the impact of your project without giving anything away on everyday performance.

  • Minéralis, distributed by MStone Surfaces: 85% first-production recycled glass and a low silica content, which is safer for those who cut and fit it.
  • MStone recycled stone: recycled minerals bound with plant-based eco-resins, for a carbon footprint up to 40% lower.
  • Compac recycled: fragments of quartz and marble, suited to projects aiming for HQE, BREEAM or LEED.
  • Ostrea: crushed oyster and scallop shells with a pearly sheen.

A responsible choice is also about lifespan. A surface chosen for how the room is really used, then cared for as we advise, will serve you for many years without needing to be replaced.

Your questions

What is the most durable worktop material?

In our comparison, ceramic (Dekton®, The Size, Infinity, MStone) gets top marks for scratches, heat, stains and upkeep. Quartzite and granite follow closely on hardness and heat, but are slightly more prone to staining. The right choice also depends on the look you want and the room in question.

Which worktop needs the least maintenance?

Non-porous surfaces are the easiest to live with: quartz, ceramic, Minéralis and MStone recycled stone clean with soapy water and need no sealing at all. If you have your heart set on natural stone, Sensa treated granites and quartzites are a good compromise. Marble, by contrast, needs sealing and more care around acidic products.

How much does a made-to-measure worktop cost?

The price depends on the material, range, thickness, cut-outs and installation, which is why we do not publish a standard rate. Send us your dimensions, a plan or a few photos. An expert will send you a free quote within 48 working hours and can price two or three materials to help you compare.
