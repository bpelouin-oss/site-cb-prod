Recycled stone: a more responsible worktop that still works hard

A recycled stone worktop gives new life to materials that would otherwise be lost: minerals, quartz and marble fragments, or shells from the food industry. We work with three very different ranges, MStone, Compac and Ostrea, and cut them to measure in our Mafra factory just like any other stone.

What is a recycled stone worktop?

Recycled stone is an engineered surface: recovered mineral materials are crushed and then bound with a resin to form slabs ready for cutting. What sets it apart is where those materials come from.

At MStone, they are recycled minerals bound with plant-based eco-resins, with no quartz or porcelain, and a carbon footprint reduced by up to 40% according to the manufacturer. At Compac, they are recycled fragments of quartz and marble. At Ostrea, they are crushed oyster and scallop shells, a food-industry waste product given a second life.

Once fitted, your worktop behaves as a fully-fledged kitchen surface, with its own qualities depending on the range.

MStone, Compac or Ostrea: comparing recycled stones

  • MStone recycled stone: non-porous and non-yellowing, with the pattern running through its full thickness. It withstands 180 °C dry heat and is cleaned with soapy water. Matt or polished finishes, 20 mm, six colours with mythological names.
  • Compac recycled stone: low porosity, resistant to scratches and knocks, with consistent colours from batch to batch. Available in 12, 20 and 30 mm, it suits projects aiming for HQE, BREEAM or LEED.
  • Ostrea: a dense, non-porous surface with a pearly fleck that shifts with the light, in a honed finish and 20 mm, in two colours, Huîtres and Saint Jacques.

If you prefer the look of quartz, Minéralis, made with 85% recycled glass, is another responsible option.

Recycled stone: pros, cons and care

These surfaces combine a more responsible approach with worry-free daily use: they are non-porous or low-porosity, need no sealing and simply clean with water and a mild soap. They also look very distinctive, especially the pearly fleck of Ostrea, which resembles no other material.

Let us be honest about the limits. The choice is narrower than with quartz or ceramic: a handful of colours per range, and a single finish for Ostrea. As with any resin-bound composite, intense heat should be avoided, so always use a trivet. For an outdoor kitchen, we would point you towards ceramic instead.

In a bathroom, on a vanity top or a piece of furniture, these materials are perfectly at home.

Your questions

Is recycled stone as tough as quartz?

For domestic use, these surfaces are designed for kitchens. Compac recycled stone resists scratches and knocks, the MStone range is non-porous and withstands 180 °C dry heat, and Ostrea forms a dense, non-porous surface. The precautions are the same as for quartz: a chopping board, a trivet for hot pans and no abrasive products.

Can recycled stone help with an HQE, BREEAM or LEED project?

Compac recycled stone is presented by its manufacturer as suitable for projects pursuing these schemes, thanks to its recycled quartz and marble fragments. For a certification file, we can pass on the manufacturer's documentation for the chosen range. Architects and project managers can also benefit from our trade prices across all our materials.

Does a recycled shell worktop smell or stain?

No. At Ostrea, oyster and scallop shells are crushed and bound with an eco-compatible resin to form a dense, non-porous surface with no odour whatsoever. Liquids do not soak in and no sealing is needed. Cleaning with water and a mild soap is enough, avoiding abrasive products that could mark the honed finish.
