Recycled stone: more responsible surfaces for your projects

A recycled stone worktop gives new value to materials that would otherwise be lost: minerals, quartz and marble fragments, or shells from the food industry. We work with three very different ranges, MStone, Compac and Ostrea, and fabricate them to measure at our Mafra factory just like any other stone, working from your drawings.

What is recycled stone?

Recycled stone is an engineered surface: recovered mineral materials are crushed and then bound with a resin to form slabs ready for cutting. What sets it apart is where those materials come from.

With MStone, they are recycled minerals bound with plant-based eco-resins, containing no quartz or porcelain, with a carbon footprint reduced by up to 40% according to the manufacturer. With Compac, they are recycled quartz and marble fragments. With Ostrea, they are crushed oyster and scallop shells, a by-product of the food industry given a second life.

Once fabricated, the worktop behaves as a kitchen surface in its own right, with its own qualities depending on the range.

MStone, Compac or Ostrea: the recycled stones we fabricate

  • MStone recycled stone: non-porous and non-yellowing, with the pattern running through the full thickness. It withstands 180 °C of dry heat. Matt or polished finishes, in 20 mm, six colours with mythological names.
  • Compac recycled stone: low porosity, resistant to scratches and knocks, with consistent colour from batch to batch. Available in 12, 20 and 30 mm, it suits projects working towards HQE, BREEAM or LEED.
  • Ostrea: a dense, non-porous surface with a pearly fleck that changes with the light, in a honed finish and 20 mm, in two colours, Huîtres and Saint Jacques.

For the look of quartz with a responsible approach, Minéralis, with 85% recycled glass, is another option to present to your clients and specifiers.

Strengths, limits and care of recycled stone

These surfaces combine a more responsible approach with worry-free daily use: they are non-porous or low-porosity, need no sealing and clean simply with water and mild soap. Their look is also highly distinctive, especially Ostrea's pearly fleck, which resembles no other material.

Some limits should be pointed out to the client. The choice is narrower than in quartz or ceramic: a few colours per range, and a single finish for Ostrea. As with any resin-bound engineered surface, intense heat should be avoided: a trivet is essential. For an outdoor kitchen, ceramic remains the better option.

In bathrooms, on a vanity top or a piece of furniture, these materials are perfectly at home.

Your questions

Is recycled stone as tough as quartz?

For domestic use, these surfaces are designed for the kitchen. Compac recycled stone resists scratches and knocks, the MStone range is non-porous and withstands 180 °C of dry heat, and Ostrea forms a dense, non-porous surface. The precautions to pass on are the same as for quartz: a chopping board, a trivet and no abrasive products.

Does recycled stone help with an HQE, BREEAM or LEED project?

Compac recycled stone is presented by its manufacturer as suitable for projects working towards these schemes, thanks to its recycled quartz and marble fragments. For a certification file, the technical documentation for the chosen range comes from the manufacturer: let us know your requirements when requesting a quote and we will point you to the right references.

Does a recycled shell worktop smell or stain?

No. With Ostrea, oyster and scallop shells are crushed and then bound with an eco-compatible resin to form a dense, non-porous surface with no odour at all. Liquids do not soak in and no sealing is needed. Cleaning with water and mild soap is enough, avoiding abrasive products that could mark the honed finish.
