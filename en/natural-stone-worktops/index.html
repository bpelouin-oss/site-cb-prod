Granite, marble and quartzite: three stones fabricated in Mafra

Natural stone is extracted from the quarry as it is, sawn into slabs and then polished. Nothing is reconstituted: no two worktops will ever be identical, which is worth keeping in mind from the design stage onwards.

We work with three families. Granite is an igneous rock with a Mohs hardness of 6 to 7, part of which comes from the quarries of Nikolaus Bagnara AG. Marble, a metamorphic rock formed from limestone, has a Mohs hardness of 3 to 4 and much sought-after veining. Quartzite, at Mohs 7, offers a veined look close to marble with far better resistance.

These stones are fabricated in 20 or 30 mm, polished or honed; depending on the colour, granite and quartzite are also available in a leathered finish, and granite in a flamed finish.

Comparing the three stones to guide your clients

The right choice depends mainly on how the room will be used and how much upkeep the end user is willing to take on.

  • Granite: excellent against heat, scratches and knocks. It is the stone for hard-working kitchens. It needs regular sealing to resist stains well.
  • Marble: the most refined, but also the softest and the most sensitive to acids. It is best suited to bathrooms, tables and statement pieces.
  • Quartzite: the veined beauty of marble with scratch and heat resistance comparable to granite. It copes better with acids and stains than marble.

If your client is torn between beauty and peace of mind, the stones treated at the factory by Cosentino, such as Sensa granite, are worth putting forward.

What natural stone means for fabrication

Stone gives a one-off piece that ages well and can be repolished. Granite and quartzite both stand up extremely well to heat and scratches, and the material suits a rustic kitchen as well as a contemporary interior.

It also has constraints that are best anticipated. Stone is porous to varying degrees: without protection, oil, wine or coffee can leave marks, especially on light colours. Marble is sensitive to lemon, vinegar and acidic products. Finally, the slab delivered may differ from the sample, since nature never repeats itself.

That is why we plan the cutting of every slab from your drawings: placement of the veins, position of cut-outs and any joints, and the best use of the material.

From your drawings to delivery: how we work

CB Prod works exclusively on fabrication and delivery. Orders are produced from the drawings, dimensions and technical information supplied by our trade customers.

  • Sending files: for example DXF, DWG or dimensioned PDF, with thickness, edge profile and cut-outs for the sink, hob and taps.
  • Technical review: checking the drawings, slab layout and cutting optimisation.
  • Fabrication: CNC cutting, machining, polishing and hand finishing.
  • Quality control: dimensions, finishes and compliance checked before dispatch.
  • Logistics: secure packaging and delivery in France, Portugal and further afield.

Site templating and on-site work remain the responsibility of you or your partners. Remember to pass on the care instructions specific to each stone to the end user.

Your questions

What information should I send for a natural stone order?

Send dimensioned drawings in a common format, for example DXF, DWG or PDF, stating the stone and colour, thickness, finish, edge profile and position of cut-outs. Specify the appliances planned, any joints you want and the delivery address. We review these details, flag anything that needs clarifying and reply with a quote within 48 working hours.

Can we see the slabs before fabrication?

Yes. Background colour, vein density and crystals vary from one block to the next, sometimes within the same slab. For a project where the pattern matters, such as a veined island or a marble vanity top, you can visit our Mafra factory by appointment, Monday to Friday from 8.00 am to 5.30 pm, on your own or with your client.

What upkeep should the end client expect from natural stone?

Granite and marble need sealing renewed at regular intervals: a drop of water that no longer beads is the sign. For daily cleaning, warm water and a mild pH-neutral soap are enough, with no bleach or scouring cream. Quartzite is more forgiving. If your client would rather not deal with it, steer them towards Cosentino's treated Sensa or Texta stones.
