What exactly is a natural stone worktop?

Natural stone is taken from the quarry as it is, then sawn into slabs and polished. Nothing is reconstituted: the pattern you see took millions of years to form, and no two worktops will ever be exactly alike.

We work with three families. Granite is an igneous rock with a Mohs hardness of 6 to 7, and part of our range comes from the quarries of Nikolaus Bagnara AG. Marble, a metamorphic rock formed from limestone, has a Mohs hardness of 3 to 4 and much sought-after veining. Quartzite, at Mohs 7, offers a veined look close to marble with far greater toughness.

All of these stones are cut in 20 or 30 mm thicknesses, polished or honed, and granite is also available with leathered and flamed finishes.

Granite, marble or quartzite: comparing the three stones

The right choice depends mainly on how the room is used and how much time you want to spend on upkeep.

  • Granite: excellent against heat, scratches and knocks. It is the stone for kitchens where people really cook. It needs regular sealing to resist stains well.
  • Marble: the most refined, but also the softest and the most sensitive to acids. We mainly recommend it for bathrooms, tables and statement pieces.
  • Quartzite: the sought-after compromise, with the veined look of marble and scratch and heat resistance comparable to granite. It copes with acids and stains better than marble.

If you are unsure, our advisers will guide you according to the way you cook and live.

Pros and cons of natural stone worktops

On the plus side, natural stone gives you a one-off piece, a material that ages gracefully and, in the case of granite and quartzite, excellent resistance to heat and scratches. It can be repaired and repolished, and it suits a rustic kitchen as well as a very contemporary interior.

The drawbacks deserve an honest word too. Stone is porous to varying degrees: without protection, oil, wine or coffee can leave marks, especially on pale colours. Marble dislikes lemon, vinegar and acidic products, which dull the polish. Finally, the slab delivered may differ slightly from the sample, because nature never repeats itself.

If you love stone but dread the upkeep, take a look at the stones treated in the factory by Cosentino, such as Sensa granite.

Caring for a natural stone worktop day to day

A well cared-for natural stone worktop will last for decades. The right habits are simple.

  • Clean with warm water and a mild pH-neutral soap, using a non-abrasive sponge.
  • Wipe up wine, oil, coffee and above all acidic liquids quickly, especially on marble.
  • Avoid bleach, descalers and scouring creams, which attack both the surface and the sealant.
  • Renew the sealing treatment at regular intervals on granite and marble: when a drop of water stops beading, it is time.

Quartzite is more forgiving but appreciates the same precautions. When we fit your worktop, we tell you which product suits your stone and its finish.

Your questions

Can I put a hot pan on a granite worktop?

Granite is among the materials that cope best with heat, as is quartzite. Setting down a hot pan now and then is generally not a problem. We still recommend a trivet for dishes straight out of the oven or for repeated heat in the same spot, which also protects the sealing treatment. On marble, which is softer, a trivet should always be used.

Does a natural stone worktop need sealing?

Yes for granite and marble: regular sealing limits how far liquids can penetrate, and therefore staining. Quartzite is tougher, but protection is still useful. If you would rather not deal with it, the stones treated in the factory by Cosentino, such as Sensa granite or Sensa quartzite, are protected against stains without regular sealing.

Will my stone slab look exactly like the sample?

Not exactly, and that is what makes natural stone so appealing. Background colour, vein density and crystals vary from block to block, sometimes within a single slab. For a project where the pattern matters a great deal, such as a veined island, you are welcome to see the materials at our Mafra factory by appointment, Monday to Friday.
