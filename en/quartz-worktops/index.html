Quartz worktops: the low-maintenance family surface

What is a quartz worktop made of?

A quartz worktop is not stone cut from a block: it is an engineered surface made in a factory, combining a very high proportion of crushed natural quartz with resins and pigments. At MStone, for example, quartz accounts for more than 93% of the material.

This make-up explains its qualities. The surface is non-porous, so liquids cannot soak in, and the pattern stays consistent from one slab to the next, which makes long runs and joins easier. Manufacturers reproduce plain colours as convincingly as highly realistic marble effects.

Be careful not to confuse it with quartzite, which is a genuine natural stone taken from a quarry, with quite different properties.

Silestone®, Compac, MStone or Stone Italiana: which quartz brand?

The four brands we work with share the qualities of quartz, but each has its own character.

  • Silestone® by Cosentino: the best known, with HybriQ® technology, polished, Suede matt and Volcano textured finishes, and a very wide choice of colours.
  • Compac: Spanish technological quartz with a Mohs hardness of 7, NSF certified, organised into the Ice Max™, Luxury, Unique and Elegance collections, in 12, 20 and 30 mm.
  • MStone: over 93% natural quartz, polished, matt, leathered and grain finishes, in 12 and 20 mm, also ideal for splashbacks and furniture.
  • Stone Italiana: a small selection of Italian quartz inspired by the most sought-after marbles and stones.

The right choice often comes down to colour and finish rather than brand.

Quartz worktops: strengths and limitations

Quartz shines in everyday life. Being non-porous, it needs no sealing and resists stains very well: coffee, wine or oil wipe away without a trace. It handles scratches and knocks well, and its even colour is reassuring when you want a predictable result.

Its main limitation comes from its resins: it copes less well with intense heat than natural stone or ceramic. A pan straight off the hob should always go on a trivet. For an outdoor kitchen exposed to the sun, we usually steer clients towards ceramic, which is designed for that use.

Finally, quartz lacks the one-off character of a stone slab: it is a choice of consistency rather than surprise.

Where quartz works best, and how to look after it

Quartz is made for family kitchens: worktops, islands, matching splashbacks and breakfast bars. It also works very well in bathrooms, as a vanity top or wall cladding, particularly in 12 mm from Compac and MStone.

Care comes down to a few simple habits:

  • a microfibre cloth or soft sponge with water and neutral soap is enough day to day;
  • for limescale marks, a gentle cleaner suitable for quartz surfaces does the job;
  • avoid very aggressive strippers, strong solvents and abrasive pads;
  • use a chopping board for cutting and a trivet for hot pans.

No treatment is needed at any point in the life of the worktop.

Your questions

Is quartz heat resistant?

Quartz handles the ordinary warmth of a kitchen, but it is less tolerant than granite or ceramic of intense, direct heat because of the resins it contains. A pan or tray straight from the oven can mark the surface. Get into the habit of using a trivet: it is the only real precaution a quartz worktop asks of you.

Can a quartz kitchen island be made without visible joins?

It depends on the size of the island and the slab format, for example 3,200 × 1,600 mm with Compac. When the island fits within one slab, there is no join on the top at all. Beyond that, our surveyors plan where the join will fall, and the even look of quartz allows a very discreet seam, cut on CNC machines to a tenth of a millimetre.

Is quartz a good choice for a kitchen with young children?

It is actually one of the best options. Its non-porous surface does not hold on to fruit juice, felt-tip pens or tomato sauce, and a wipe with a sponge is enough. It stands up well to everyday knocks and scratches. The only precaution is to put hot dishes on a trivet. A lightly speckled or veined colour also hides small marks between cleans.
