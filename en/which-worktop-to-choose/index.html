Choosing the right worktop for the way you live

Which worktop to choose when every material promises beauty and strength? The right answer depends above all on how you live: how hard your kitchen works, how much care you will give it, the look you want and what matters to you. This guide and the comparison below help you narrow it down.

Which worktop to choose for a hard-working kitchen

If you cook every day, pans go straight from hob to worktop and the children do their homework there, choose a forgiving material.

Ceramic, such as Dekton®, The Size or Infinity, scores highest in our comparison: it resists scratches, heat and stains, and absorbs almost no water. Granite and quartzite, both very hard natural stones, also cope extremely well with heat and knocks, with slightly more attentive care. The treated Sensa versions reduce that need.

Quartz is an excellent family surface, non-porous and easy to clean, but it is less tolerant of direct heat, so a trivet soon becomes a habit. Marble and Corian® are still possible, as long as you are happy for them to develop a patina over time.

Balancing budget and appearance

The price of a worktop does not depend on the material alone. The range and colour, thickness, finish, total surface area and number of cut-outs weigh just as much.

A few ways to keep your budget under control without giving up on the look:

  • keep the most striking material for the island or splashback, and choose a quieter surface for the main run;
  • check the thicknesses each range offers, as some come in 12, 20 or 30 mm;
  • compare two families with a similar look, for example a natural stone and an engineered surface inspired by it;
  • avoid complex cut-outs that add little real convenience.

Share your budget with your adviser from the outset: they can then present several sensible options rather than a single quote that is hard to adjust.

The marble look without marble's drawbacks

Marble wins hearts with its veining, but it is a softer stone that reacts to acids such as lemon juice or vinegar. In a kitchen, several materials give a similar look with fewer precautions.

Quartzite is often the first option: a natural stone harder than marble, it shares its veining and stands up better to acids and stains. The Quartzite Sensa collection adds a factory-applied stain protection.

Large-format ceramics faithfully reproduce the great marbles, with slabs that keep joints to a minimum on an island or splashback. Some veined quartzes play the same card, with a non-porous surface.

If you have your heart set on real marble, Marbre Texta receives a surface treatment that improves its resilience. And marble remains perfect in a bathroom or as a table.

A more responsible worktop choice

If the impact of your project matters to you, several materials deserve a closer look.

Minéralis, distributed by MStone Surfaces, gives the look of quartz with 85% first-production recycled glass and a low silica content, which also makes it safer to work for the people who cut it. It withstands 180 °C and carries a 20-year warranty.

Our recycled stones follow the same path. MStone recycled stone combines recycled minerals with plant-based eco-resins. Compac's version incorporates recycled fragments of quartz and marble. Ostrea makes use of crushed oyster and scallop shells, which create a speckled pattern with a pearly sheen.

Finally, a well-chosen, well-maintained worktop lasts for many years: durability remains the first responsible choice.

Your questions

Which worktop is the most heat resistant?

Ceramic is the most tolerant: made from minerals sintered at very high temperatures, it can take a hot pan without damage. Granite and quartzite also resist heat very well. Quartz, Corian® and marble need more care. As a precaution, a trivet is still advisable on every surface, in line with the manufacturer's recommendations.

Which worktop is best for a bathroom?

A bathroom sees fewer knocks and less heat than a kitchen, which widens the choice. Marble really comes into its own here. Corian® allows seamless integrated basins and curved shapes. Quartz, ceramic and Minéralis also work very well, thanks to their non-porous, easy-to-clean surfaces.

What thickness should a worktop be?

It depends on the material, the style and the structure of your units. A slim profile gives a refined, contemporary look, popular with ceramic. A thicker one gives presence to an island or a natural stone. Available thicknesses vary from range to range, and our advisers will tell you which suit your project.
