Inspiration

Why choose Stone Italiana for your worktop

Stone Italiana is for people who want a carefully designed quartz without getting lost in a palette of dozens of shades. Our four references cover the essentials: two white marble effects, an absolute white and a soft grey.

Day to day, you get all the qualities of quartz: a surface that cleans with a wipe of a sponge, needs no treatment and stands up well to the stains of family life. Like any quartz, it appreciates a trivet for scorching pans and a board for cutting.

Thicknesses of 20 and 30 mm and polished or matt finishes, depending on the colour, let you adapt the worktop to the style of the kitchen, from the most classic to the most minimal.

Choosing your Stone Italiana colour

Calacatta Extra and Statuario Venato are the two marble effects in the selection. The first has broad grey and gold veins, perfect for a central island or a kitchen with brass details. The second, with more pronounced veining, pairs well with black, grey or dark wood fronts for a strong contrast.

Bianco Assoluto is an even, vein-free white, ideal for a minimalist kitchen or a splashback that should fade into the background. Grigio Seta, a silky grey with soft nuances, suits contemporary kitchens and hides everyday marks well.

These two plain shades come in polished and matt, while the marble effects are offered polished: a detail worth checking in the catalogue below before you decide.

Your questions

Stone Italiana or Silestone®: what is the difference?

Both are quartz surfaces with comparable everyday properties: non-porous, no treatment needed, to be protected from intense heat. The difference lies mainly in the offer. Silestone®, by Cosentino, has a very wide palette and three finishes. Stone Italiana, an Italian manufacturer, is available from us as a short selection of four shades. Start with the colour you like best.

Is a marble-effect quartz more practical than real marble?

In a kitchen, yes. A quartz such as Calacatta Extra or Statuario Venato is not bothered by lemon or vinegar, needs no sealing and scratches less than marble. On the other hand, you lose the one-off character of stone and its patina. Many of our clients choose quartz for the kitchen and keep real marble for the bathroom.
