Your kitchen worktop, made to your exact measurements

Which materials suit a kitchen worktop?

In a kitchen, the worktop works harder than anywhere else. These are the families we recommend most often:

  • ceramic (Dekton®, Infinity, MStone) for a hard-working kitchen: it copes with heat, scratches and stains;
  • granite, for heat and knocks, with regular sealing, or the Sensa treated version to do without it;
  • quartz, non-porous and popular with families for its minimal upkeep, with a trivet for very hot pans;
  • Minéralis, which offers the look of quartz with 85% recycled glass and heat resistance up to 180 °C.

Dreaming of marble? Bear in mind that it remains sensitive to lemon, vinegar and wine. A quartzite or a marble-effect ceramic gives you similar veining with far fewer precautions day to day.

Sink and hob cut-outs, built-in details

Every opening is recorded by our surveyors, then CNC-machined to a tenth of a millimetre. Depending on the material, several options are possible:

  • an undermounted sink, fixed beneath the stone so you can wipe the worktop straight into the bowl;
  • a sink carved from the solid, made directly in the material;
  • drainer grooves cut beside the bowl;
  • a hob cut-out matched to the model you have chosen;
  • holes for the tap, a socket or a soap dispenser.

The edges of each cut-out are then finished by hand for a clean result that is smooth to the touch. To save time, include the model references of your sink and hob in your quote request: we work from the dimensions supplied by their manufacturers.

Thicknesses, edges and surface finishes

Thickness changes both the look of the kitchen and how the worktop is fitted. Depending on the range, we work from 12 mm, slim and contemporary, up to a more generous 20 or 30 mm; some ceramics even come in 8 mm. Depending on the material, a slim worktop can also be given a thickened edge for a solid look without adding weight to the structure.

Next comes the edge profile: crisp and square, slightly rounded to feel softer, or chamfered. Every edge is finished by hand in our workshop after digital cutting.

Finally, the surface finish. High-gloss polish brings out the depth of the colours. Honed or matt finishes look more understated. Leathered and textured finishes feel tactile and show fewer fingerprints and water marks.

A splashback to match your worktop

Continuing the worktop up the wall in the same material gives a kitchen that reads as one piece, with no break in colour or veining. That was the idea behind one of our projects in Breccia Sarda marble, where worktop and splashback share the same cream tone, set against sand-beige and dark wood units.

The splashback can be a simple upstand a few centimetres high or cover the whole wall between the worktop and the wall units. Behind the hob, choose a surface that handles heat well and is easy to clean, such as ceramic. We cut openings for sockets and switches to measure, and the splashback is fitted straight after the worktop. To find out more, see our dedicated kitchen splashback page.

From survey to installation of your bespoke kitchen worktop

It all starts with your request: a few lines, plans or photos are enough. An expert reviews it, calls you back if anything is missing, then sends you a free quote within 48 working hours.

Once your base units are in place, our surveyors visit to record every angle, wall and cut-out. Manufacturing begins from that survey in our Mafra workshop, where each piece is CNC-cut and then finished by hand.

Our teams then deliver and install your worktop, in France or in Portugal. Before they leave, they give you care advice specific to your material. Already working with a kitchen company or a fitter? Supply only is available, and trade professionals benefit from preferential pricing.

Your questions

What thickness should a kitchen worktop be?

20 mm, offered in most of our ranges, suits the majority of kitchens. 30 mm gives a more substantial look that is often popular on an island. 12 mm, available in ceramic and some quartz ranges, creates a very slim, contemporary line. Our advisers will check compatibility with your sink, hob and units.

Can you put a hot pan on a quartz worktop?

Quartz copes with everyday warmth, but a pan straight off the heat can mark it, so keep a trivet to hand. If you often put scorching dishes down on the worktop, ceramic and granite are more forgiving. Minéralis, for its part, is rated heat resistant up to 180 °C.

Should you choose the worktop before or after the units?

It is best to choose the material early, as it affects the cut-outs, the type of sink and sometimes even the choice of door fronts. The final survey, however, takes place on site once the base units are fitted and levelled. Our surveyors then take exact measurements, so the worktop fits your actual walls.

Does a granite worktop need sealing?

Natural granite benefits from regular sealing, which stops grease and coloured stains from soaking in. We hand over the right care advice on installation. If you would rather avoid this, Sensa by Cosentino treated granites offer improved stain resistance without regular sealing, while keeping their natural look.
