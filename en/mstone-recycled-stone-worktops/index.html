Inspiration

Why choose MStone recycled stone for your worktop

This composite is for people who want to reduce the impact of their kitchen without giving up everyday comfort. According to MStone Surfaces, its carbon footprint can be up to 40% lower, and the absence of quartz makes it a different material from conventional quartz.

Day to day, it is easy to live with: being non-porous, it needs no treatment, and it is described as non-yellowing, which matters for pale colours. It withstands 180 °C dry heat; beyond that, a trivet is a must.

Its pattern runs through the full thickness. In practice, the edges, sink and hob cut-outs and any small scuffs keep the same look as the surface, with no different layer showing through.

Choosing your MStone recycled stone colour

The six colours are split between bright pale tones, sandy hues and deeper shades.

  • Apollon: a greyish off-white with soft clouds and fine taupe flecks, for a light, natural kitchen.
  • Orion: an ivory white with delicate diagonal beige-grey veins, more elegant.
  • Athena: a greige scattered with fine particles, very easy to pair with wood.
  • Hyperion: a deep sandy beige leaning towards camel, warm and tone on tone.
  • Athos: an even mid-grey with a very fine grain, for a sober, contemporary look.
  • Helios: a slate-like anthracite crossed by fine white veins.

Two finishes are possible, matt or polished, in 20 mm. Matt emphasises the raw mineral look, polished brings out the flecks.

Your questions

What does non-yellowing mean for a worktop?

Some resin-bound surfaces can take on a slightly yellow tint over time under the effect of light. MStone recycled stone is described as non-yellowing by its manufacturer: its pale colours, such as Apollon or Orion, are designed to keep their shade. That is an important criterion if your kitchen is very bright or you are choosing a white.

How do I care for an MStone recycled stone worktop?

It is one of the easiest to look after: soapy water and a soft sponge are enough day to day, with no sealing at all. Wipe up spills promptly, avoid harsh strippers and abrasive pads, and always put scorching pans on a trivet, as the stated resistance is 180 °C dry heat. A chopping board protects the surface from blades.
