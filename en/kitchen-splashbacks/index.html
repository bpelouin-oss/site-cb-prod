A splashback that carries your worktop up the wall

A kitchen splashback protects the wall from grease and water, but it is also one of the most visible surfaces in the room. Made from large panels cut to measure, without the grout lines of tiling, it either continues your worktop up the wall or sets it off by contrast.

Which materials make the best kitchen splashback?

A splashback does not take the same punishment as a worktop: nothing is chopped on it, but it does catch splashes and, behind the hob, heat. The materials we recommend:

  • ceramic (Dekton®, MStone, Infinity), non-porous and heat tolerant, ideal behind a hob; MStone's 12 mm is perfect on walls and Dekton® comes in 8 mm;
  • The Size ceramic, available in 6 mm, for a very slim splashback;
  • MStone quartz and Minéralis, uniform and easy to clean;
  • marble and quartzite, for a striking splashback, with marble needing to be sealed.

The choice is usually made at the same time as the worktop: the same material for continuity, or a deliberate contrast to give the kitchen more depth.

Matching or contrasting splashback

A matching splashback, cut from the same material as the worktop, gives the kitchen a very clean look. With a veined stone, the effect is even stronger when the veining runs on from worktop to wall. Several of our projects take this approach: a Calacatta Vagli Rosato marble splashback above a white worktop, or cream Breccia Sarda marble for both worktop and splashback.

Contrast works just as well: a plain worktop with a heavily veined splashback, or the other way round. In terms of height, you can choose a simple upstand a few centimetres high, a splashback between the worktop and the wall units, or full-height cladding that turns the wall into a stone feature. One example is a wall panel in Black Tempest quartzite, black veined with gold, paired with fluted dark wood fronts.

Fitting a kitchen splashback: precision and cut-outs

A splashback made from large panels cannot be adjusted on site the way tiles can: it has to be right from the moment it is made. Our surveyors therefore record the actual dimensions of the wall, any variations from plumb and the position of every socket, switch and extractor outlet.

In our Mafra workshop, the panels are CNC-cut to a tenth of a millimetre, openings included, then the visible edges are finished by hand. Our teams then fit the splashback together with the worktop or on its own, in France or in Portugal.

Renovating without changing the worktop? A splashback on its own is perfectly possible, as long as the material works with what is already there. Send us the dimensions and a few photos of the wall, and an expert will send you a free quote within 48 working hours.

Your questions

How thick should a kitchen splashback be?

A splashback does not need to be as thick as a worktop. Depending on the range, we offer ceramics from 6 to 12 mm, which are light and ideal on walls, or the same material as the worktop in 12 or 20 mm for seamless continuity. Our advisers will guide you according to the wall and the effect you want.

Can you have a stone splashback behind a hob?

Yes, provided you choose a material that copes with heat and splashes. Ceramic is the safest bet: it resists heat, is unaffected by grease and cleans without special products. Granite also works well. With quartz or marble, follow the clearances recommended by your hob manufacturer.

Is a stone splashback hard to look after?

With no grout lines, a large-panel splashback is easier to look after than tiles. Ceramic, quartz and Minéralis clean with soapy water and need no treatment. Marble needs sealing and gentle products, as it reacts to acids such as lemon or vinegar. We give you the right care advice when we install it.
