Quartzite, the veined stone that is at home in the kitchen

Dreaming of marble veining but cooking every day? A quartzite worktop is often the answer. This natural stone, with a Mohs hardness of 7, offers spectacular and sometimes translucent patterns, with toughness comparable to granite. We offer 37 colours, cut to measure in Mafra.

Worktops in Quartzite

Harder than marble (Mohs 7), quartzite combines the veined beauty of marble with the strength of granite. It also stands up better to acids and stains.

Quartzite vs marble: the advantage

  • Mohs hardness 7
  • Greater resistance to stains and acids
  • Comparable veined look
  • Easier upkeep

Our projects

Why choose quartzite for your worktop

Quartzite forms when sandstone is transformed by heat and pressure: its quartz grains fuse into a very compact rock. As a result, it earns top marks in our selection guide for scratches and heat, and very good marks for stains and upkeep.

That is the key difference from marble, whose veined look it shares: quartzite copes with cutting, hot pans and everyday acids far more calmly. Sealing is still useful, especially on pale colours, but the stone is much more forgiving.

It should not be confused with quartz, an engineered surface made in a factory. Quartzite comes out of the quarry, and every slab is unique.

Choosing your quartzite colour

The 37 quartzites in our catalogue cover very different moods.

  • For a bright, light kitchen: Mont Blanc, White Orion or Acquabella, with gentle veining.
  • For a spectacular effect: Patagonia Crystal with its crystalline golden veins, or Spectrum with its iridescent hues, best kept for an island or a worktop that will be the room's focal point.
  • For a rare blue: Macaubas, a much sought-after blue and white Brazilian quartzite.
  • For a darker mood: Crystal Smoke, a smoky grey with translucent veins, or Black Tempest.

Some crystalline quartzites let light through, which opens the door to backlit effects. Come and see the slabs before choosing: with these stones, the actual pattern matters more than ever.

Your questions

Does quartzite stain?

Far less than marble, and generally less than granite, but it is still a natural stone and its porosity varies from one variety to another. On a pale quartzite, oil left for a long time can leave a mark. Sealing at installation, renewed from time to time, and quick wiping are enough. To stop worrying about it altogether, Sensa quartzite is protected in the factory by Cosentino.

Can a quartzite worktop be backlit?

Some crystalline quartzites, such as Aura Crystal or Patagonia Crystal, let some light through, making beautiful effects possible on a bar or an island front. This is not true of every variety, and the result depends on the thickness and on the slab itself. We assess feasibility case by case, ideally by testing the slab you have chosen.
