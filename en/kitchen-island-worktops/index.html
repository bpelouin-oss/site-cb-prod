Kitchen islands: large-format tops made to your drawings

A kitchen island worktop is seen from every side: every edge, joint and vein counts. For kitchen studios, interior contractors and architects, we manufacture large-format island tops, with or without waterfall ends and sometimes extended into a table, from your drawings, then deliver them from our factory in Mafra.

Large formats: fewer joints, or none

On an island, a single-piece top is often the ideal. It all depends on the size of the available slabs:

  • Dekton® in Jumbo format, up to 3,300 × 1,630 mm;
  • Minéralis and The Size, in 3,200 × 1,600 mm and 3,200 × 1,500 mm slabs;
  • natural stone, whose size varies with the block taken from the quarry.

When the island is longer than the slab, a joint is needed. When we study your drawings, we suggest placing it where it will be least noticeable, often in line with the hob or sink, while matching the veining. Let us know about site access constraints too, such as corridors, stairs or narrow doors: they sometimes determine how pieces are split and packed.

Waterfall ends, table extensions and edges

Waterfall ends, or end panels, carry the top down to the floor on one or both sides. For the veining to flow from one face to the next, top and ends are usually cut from the same slab and prepared for a mitred joint. A table extension can continue in the same material at the same height, or drop to dining height: show on your drawings the overhang length and the planned support, whether legs, a frame, a stone end panel or a concealed structure.

On an island, edges stay visible all the way round. A straight edge underlines an architectural line; a softened or rounded profile is kinder to passers-by. In 30 mm, or with a thickened edge, the top gains presence. Every edge is finished by hand after CNC cutting.

Which material for a kitchen island top?

An island often combines cooking, washing up and dining, so the material has to cope with a bit of everything. Ceramic and quartzite, both very tough, suit the busiest islands, and ceramic offers the largest formats. Quartz and Minéralis appeal for their consistent look from one slab to the next, valuable when two slabs must be joined. Granite, unique in every block, brings character and handles heat well.

For the dining area, favour a surface that resists staining, such as quartz, ceramic or a treated stone. For a mainly decorative island, marble or a Texta treated quartzite can also work. On such a large surface, a full slab is far easier to judge than a sample: come and see it with your client at our Mafra factory, by appointment.

Your questions

What is the maximum length for a seamless island top?

It depends on the slab. The largest in our catalogue are Dekton® ceramics in Jumbo format, up to 3,300 × 1,630 mm, followed by Minéralis and The Size at around 3,200 mm long. Beyond that, a joint is needed: when we study your drawings, we suggest the most discreet position for it.

Can you include a hob and sink cut-out in the island?

Yes, it is very common. Cut-outs are machined to the hob and sink references shown on your drawings, allowing for the service routes planned inside the island. Choose a surface that tolerates heat and splashes, such as ceramic, granite or quartzite, and leave a clear zone between the cooking and dining areas.

Does an island with waterfall ends cost more?

Waterfall ends require more material and careful preparation of the mitres, which shows in the quote. To keep your client's budget in check, a single end on the most visible side may be enough. Send us the island drawings: we will price the options you are considering within 48 working hours.
