An island worktop designed as the centrepiece

Kitchen island worktops are seen from every side, so each edge, joint and vein matters. We design yours with you in large format, with or without waterfall ends and sometimes extended into a table, then make it in our factory in Mafra before installing it in your home.

Large formats: fewer joints, or none at all

On an island, a single-piece top is often ideal. It all depends on the size of the slabs available:

  • Dekton® in Jumbo format, up to 3,300 × 1,630 mm;
  • Minéralis and The Size, in slabs of 3,200 × 1,600 mm and 3,200 × 1,500 mm;
  • natural stones, whose size varies with the block extracted from the quarry.

When the island is longer than the slab, a joint is needed. We place it where it shows least, often in line with the hob opening or sink cut-out, and aim to match the veining across it. Think too about the route the slab must take to reach your kitchen: corridors, stairs and doorways sometimes dictate how it is divided. Let us know about any constraints and we will allow for them from the survey onwards.

Extending the island into a table

An island extended into a table becomes the heart of the home: a place to cook, eat and work. The top can continue in the same material at the same height, or step down to table height for a more comfortable dining area. One of our projects pairs an island with rounded corners, extended into a table, with a matching splashback in Calacatta Vagli Rosato marble.

The overhanging section needs the right support: legs, a frame, a stone end panel or a concealed structure, depending on the length of the overhang and the material. We agree the solution with you or your kitchen designer. For the dining area, choose a surface that resists stains, such as quartz, ceramic or treated stone, because that is where wine and coffee get spilled.

Waterfall ends and edges: the finish on show

Waterfall ends carry the top down to the floor on one or both sides. They protect the sides of the units and give the island the look of a sculpted block. For the best effect, the top and waterfall ends are usually cut from the same slab and mitred, so the veining flows from one face to the next.

On an island, the edges are visible all the way round. A square edge emphasises a clean architectural line, while a softened or rounded profile is kinder to walk past, especially with young children. At 30 mm, or with a thickened edge, the worktop gains presence. Every edge is finished by hand in our workshop after CNC cutting, for a consistent result along its whole length.

Which material for kitchen island worktops?

An island often combines cooking, washing up and dining, so the material has to cope with a bit of everything. Ceramic and quartzite, both very tough, suit the busiest islands, and ceramic offers the largest formats. Quartz and Minéralis appeal for their consistent appearance from slab to slab, which is valuable when two slabs have to be joined. Granite, unique in every block, brings character and handles heat well.

For a mainly decorative island or a breakfast bar, a marble or a Texta treated quartzite can also work. Come and compare the slabs in person at our factory in Mafra, by appointment: on a surface this large, a full slab is far easier to judge than a small sample.

Your questions

How long can an island worktop be without a joint?

It depends on the slab. The largest in our catalogue are Dekton® ceramics in Jumbo format, up to 3,300 × 1,630 mm, followed by Minéralis and The Size at around 3,200 mm long. Beyond that a joint is needed, and we agree its position with you so that it is as discreet as possible.

Can you put a hob on a kitchen island?

Yes, it is very common. The cut-out is made to measure from the hob model, taking into account the extractor and the electrical supply planned in the island. In that case, choose a surface that tolerates heat and splashes, such as ceramic, granite or quartzite, and keep a clear zone between the cooking area and the dining side.

Does an island with waterfall ends cost more?

Waterfall ends use more material and need careful edge joining, which is reflected in the quote. To keep the budget in check, a single waterfall end on the most visible side can be enough.
