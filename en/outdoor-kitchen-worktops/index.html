An outdoor kitchen built to take the sun

Outdoor kitchen worktops live outside all year round, facing sun, rain, temperature swings and barbecue grease. Not every material is up to it. We point you towards surfaces suited to the job, ceramic and granite, then craft them in Mafra and install them in France and Portugal.

Ceramic: the go-to material for outdoor kitchen worktops

Ultra-compact ceramic is the solution we recommend most often outdoors. Dekton®, made by sintering natural minerals with Cosentino's TSP process, has almost zero water absorption and high resistance to UV, heat, scratches and stains. MStone ceramic, non-porous and treatment-free, is unaffected by sunlight, and Infinity is even used on building façades.

In practice, this means:

  • colours that stay stable in the sun;
  • a hot dish put down without fear of marking;
  • grease and wine stains that come off with soapy water.

Frost resistance depends on the specific product and how exposed it is. We check the manufacturer's recommendations with you, which is essential if your outdoor kitchen has to get through harsh winters.

Granite: a natural stone made for the outdoors

A very hard igneous rock, granite handles heat, scratches and knocks well, which is invaluable around a barbecue. Every slab is unique, extracted from the quarries of our partner Nikolaus Bagnara, from deep Nero Assoluto Zimbabwe to the lighter Silver Cloud.

Outdoors, the finish matters as much as the stone. Flamed granite, produced by exposing the surface to intense heat, has a rough, less slippery texture that works well on worktops exposed to rain. Leathered granite, softer to the touch, hides marks well.

Natural granite remains slightly porous, so sealing is recommended to limit grease stains, and Sensa treated versions make upkeep easier. Finally, bear in mind that a very dark stone gets hotter in full sun than a lighter shade, something to consider on a south-facing terrace.

Designing your made-to-measure outdoor worktop

An outdoor kitchen should be planned like a real kitchen: a cooking zone, a sink, a preparation area and often a counter for serving. We cut openings to measure for a built-in barbecue, plancha or sink, and can clad the sides to create a single, solid unit.

A few tips before you choose:

  • in full sun, avoid quartz and resin-bound surfaces, which are more sensitive to UV than ceramic;
  • go for a matt or textured finish, which is less slippery and less glaring in direct light;
  • allow for a slight fall or a drip edge so that rainwater does not sit on the worktop.

Send us plans or photos of your terrace. An expert will review your project and send you a free quote within 48 working hours, with installation or supply only.

Your questions

What is the best material for an outdoor kitchen worktop?

Ultra-compact ceramic such as Dekton® is the most versatile choice outdoors: almost zero water absorption and resistance to UV, heat and stains. Granite is an excellent natural alternative, especially with a flamed or leathered finish. For frost, we check the manufacturer's recommendations with you for the chosen product and your climate.

Can a quartz worktop be used outdoors?

It is not recommended in full sun. Quartz contains resins that can react to UV over time and alter its colour. Under a fully covered shelter the situation is different, but ceramic remains the safer choice for an outdoor kitchen. Tell us how exposed your terrace is and we will advise you case by case.

How do you look after an outdoor worktop?

With ceramic, soapy water is all you need, even after a barbecue, and no treatment is required. Granite cleans the same way but benefits from regular sealing to limit grease stains. In both cases, do not leave leaves or standing water on the surface, and cover the kitchen if it goes unused over the winter.
