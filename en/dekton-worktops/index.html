Inspiration

Why choose Dekton® for your worktop

Dekton® is often chosen by people who refuse to compromise on everyday use. Its high resistance to heat, scratches, stains and UV, and its near-zero water absorption, make it one of the most forgiving surfaces in our catalogue. No sealing is ever needed, now or in ten years' time.

The Jumbo format, 3,300 × 1,630 mm, makes it possible to create large islands in a single piece. The range of thicknesses, from 8 to 30 mm, opens up every option: a substantial worktop, a slim contemporary top, a splashback, cabinet fronts or wall cladding.

It is also the material we recommend for an outdoor kitchen, since open-air use is one of its intended applications.

Choosing your Dekton® colour and finish

Dekton® colours are grouped into four families, which helps you find your way.

  • Natural: veins and stones, such as Sirius, a veined white, Trevi or Laurent.
  • Solid: pure plain tones, such as Zenith, or the metal effect of Liquid Steel.
  • Tech: concrete and urban materials, such as Keon or Keranium, a dark polished concrete.
  • Industrial: oxidation and patina, for kitchens with strong character.

The finish matters as much as the colour. X-Gloss gives a mirror polish that heightens the veins, matt has a natural stone look, and Velvet a softer feel. In a busy kitchen, matt hides fingerprints better. Not every finish is offered for every colour: the catalogue below shows which.

Your questions

What Dekton® thickness should I choose for a worktop?

Dekton® comes in 8, 12, 20 and 30 mm. The thinner options are mainly used for splashbacks, fronts and wall cladding, and 12 mm also allows a very slim worktop on a suitable support. 20 mm is the most common choice in kitchens, and 30 mm gives a solid look. Our surveyors confirm the thickness based on your layout and any overhangs.

Can I cut directly on a Dekton® worktop?

The Dekton® surface is highly scratch resistant, and a knife will generally not mark it. Your knife is more likely to suffer: such a hard material quickly blunts blades. We therefore recommend keeping a chopping board, as much for your knives as for comfort. If a metal mark appears on a matt finish, it comes off with a suitable cleaner.
